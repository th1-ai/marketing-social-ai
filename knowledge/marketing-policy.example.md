# Marketing policy - Hotel Aurora (example)

This is sample data showing the shape and level of detail worth writing.
Copy it to `knowledge/marketing-policy.md` and replace every line with your
own property's answer. The copilot (`tools/copilot.py`) and the two
narration prompts (`prompts/budget_note.md`, `prompts/campaign_note.md`)
read this file — it is the closest thing this agent has to a house style
guide.

## Voice

- Plain, specific, never salesy. One idea per sentence.
- Sentence-case headlines, not Title Case.
- CTAs are verbs: "Book the winter escape," "Reserve your ritual" — never
  "Click here."
- No exclamation marks. No countdown timers. No "Limited time only" unless
  the offer genuinely has an end date, stated plainly.

## Claims we never make

- No unverifiable superlatives ("the best spa on the coast"). If we cannot
  back a claim with a fact, we do not make it.
- No discounting language dressed up as urgency ("Act now, prices rising!").
  One offer per creative, stated once, plainly.
- No claims about a competitor.

## Budget philosophy

- We would rather pause a losing ad a week early than let it run "to see
  what happens." €900 in 30 days with nothing to show for it is not a test
  worth continuing.
- We test one variable at a time (CTA colour, then copy, then photo) so we
  actually learn which change worked.
- Brand-term search (our own name) is demand-capped, not growth — we do not
  chase clicks we would have gotten for free.

## Review-quote ads

- Quote guests accurately, in context. Never edit a quote to imply
  something the guest did not say.
- Attribute honestly: "a recent guest," not a fabricated name, unless the
  guest has given explicit permission to use their name.

## Seasonal notes

- Shoulder season (April-May, October) leans on quiet/wellness framing, not
  discount framing — we are not empty, we are peaceful.
- Peak season (June-September) never discounts; scarcity is real, so we
  lead with availability, not price.
